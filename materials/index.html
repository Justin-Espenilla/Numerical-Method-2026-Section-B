Numerical Methods Laboratory

Ten laboratory sessions built on the Week 1–2 lecture topics — derivatives, integrals, matrices, infinite series and Taylor series — applied to problems a civil engineer meets in practice: reservoir records, beam deflections, river discharge, trusses, seepage, consolidation settlement and channel design.

Python 3, NumPy, Matplotlib 10 lab lectures · 10 assignments Worked lecture programs and starter files AI use permitted, prompts graded

How the course is organised

EACH SESSION

The lab lecture

A civil engineering problem, the method it forces you to build, one fully worked example carried through by hand and by program, and the conditions under which the method fails.

EACH WEEK

The assignment

Four or five parts: implement, verify, apply, interpret. Data is seeded from your student number, so every submission is individual.

EVERY SUBMISSION

The AI Prompt Log

A Word document recording every prompt used, word for word, with the checks you ran and the defects you found. Worth 15–30 % of each assignment.

Policy in one paragraph

Using an AI assistant is permitted throughout this course. Concealing that use is academic dishonesty. A student who writes a precise prompt, tests the answer against an exact value, finds the defect and corrects it will score higher than a student who produced the same code alone and documented nothing. The full policy and the four-level prompt rubric are in the grading system.

What a student can do at the end

  1. Take a civil engineering problem, write its governing equation, and identify the numerical method it requires.
  2. Implement that method from first principles rather than calling a library and hoping.
  3. State the error in the answer and defend the step size, mesh or tolerance that produced it.
  4. Predict where a method will fail before it fails on a project.
  5. Direct an AI assistant precisely, verify what it returns, and correct it in writing.
