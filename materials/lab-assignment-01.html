Part A — Build and verify 30 pts

  1. Implement derivative(x, y) exactly as specified in the lab: centred in the interior, three-point one-sided (O(h2)) at both endpoints, returning an array the same length as y. Raise a clear error for unequally spaced x.
  2. Implement second_derivative(x, y) for the interior points.
  3. Prove the order. For f(x) = x3 − 2x at x = 1.5, tabulate the true relative error of the forward and centred formulas for h = 0.2, 0.1, 0.05, 0.025, 0.0125. Add a column giving the ratio of consecutive errors. State what each ratio column converges to and why.
  4. On one log-log plot, show both error curves against h, and fit a straight line to each to measure the slope numerically. Report the measured slopes to two decimals and compare them to the theoretical orders.

Part B — A physical record 30 pts

Generate the dataset with the code below. It simulates a reservoir stage recorder at a flood-control dam, sampling every 5 minutes for 24 hours, with the quantisation and sensor noise of a real stilling-well float gauge. Use seed = the last two digits of your student number, so the record is your own.

import numpy as np

def reservoir_data(seed):
    """Stage record at a flood-control dam. t in hours, h in metres."""
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    t   = np.arange(0, 24.0001, 1/12)                   # hours, every 5 min
    h   = 12 + 3.2/(1 + np.exp(-(t - 10)/1.6)) \
             - 0.02*t + 0.15*np.sin(2*np.pi*t/6)          # metres
    h  += rng.normal(0, 0.015, t.size)                    # float-gauge noise
    h   = np.round(h, 2)                                  # recorder rounds to the cm
    return t, h
  1. Plot depth against time. Then plot the raw dh/dt from your differentiator on a second axis. Describe in two sentences what is wrong with the second plot as an engineering result.
  2. Quantify the damage. The recorder rounds to 0.01 m, the float gauge adds σ = 0.015 m, and the sampling interval is 1/12 h. Using the noise-amplification reasoning from the lab, predict the standard deviation of the spurious oscillation in dh/dt before looking at your plot. Then measure it as the standard deviation of (raw derivative − smoothed derivative). Agreement within a factor of two is a pass.
  3. Apply a centred moving average, sweeping the window from 3 to 25 samples. For each window, record the peak inflow rate and the time at which it occurs. Present this as a table. Handle the ends of the record by edge padding, and state how you did it.
  4. Make the call. Choose one window length. Report the peak filling rate in m³/s (take the reservoir surface area as 4.5 × 105 m²) and the clock time of the peak. Then defend your window in a short paragraph: what does a shorter window include that you do not want, and what does a longer window destroy? For reference, the true underlying peak of the generating function is 0.50 m/h at t = 10 h before the 6-hour oscillation is added.
  5. Compute the second derivative of the smoothed record and mark the inflection point on your figure. State its physical meaning for the flood-control operator in plain language.
Where marks are lost in Part B

Reporting a peak rate to four decimal places from data quantised at the centimetre. Your answer cannot be more precise than your instrument. State the number of significant figures your result actually supports, and justify it.

Part C — Sensitivity preview 25 pts

The terminal velocity of a falling body with quadratic drag is

vt = √ 2mgρCdA

with m = 75 kg, g = 9.81 m/s², ρ = 1.20 kg/m³, Cd = 1.15, A = 0.70 m².

  1. Compute each partial derivative ∂vt/∂(parameter) numerically, using a centred difference with a perturbation of 1 % of each parameter.
  2. Derive at least two of them analytically by hand and confirm your numerical values agree to at least four significant figures. Show the hand derivation in the report.
  3. Convert each to a dimensionless sensitivity S = (∂vt/∂p)·(p/vt) and rank the five parameters. Which one would you spend money measuring more precisely, and which would you not bother with? One paragraph.
  4. If Cd is known only to ±10 %, what is the resulting uncertainty in vt? Answer using your sensitivity, then verify by direct evaluation at both extremes.
