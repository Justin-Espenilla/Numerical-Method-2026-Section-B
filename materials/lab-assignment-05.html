Part A — River discharge 30 pts

A gauging team returns with the following survey. Stations are unevenly spaced, because the bank was not accessible at regular intervals.

x (m)0.00.82.03.55.06.27.59.010.411.512.0
H (m)0.000.350.921.481.852.051.981.621.050.420.00
U (m/s)0.000.220.550.841.061.181.140.930.640.280.00
  1. Before writing code, state the integrand for discharge and carry out the unit check in the report. One line each for cross-sectional area, discharge, and mean velocity.
  2. Compute the cross-sectional area A, the discharge Q, and the discharge-weighted mean velocity Q/A, using a trapezoidal rule that handles unequal spacing.
  3. Compute the unweighted arithmetic mean of the velocity column. Report the percentage difference from Q/A, state which is physically correct, and explain the sign of the difference in terms of where the deep water sits.
  4. Locate the centroid of the cross-section, x̄. Plot the channel profile with the centroid marked, and state whether the section is symmetric.
  5. Estimate the error in Q by Richardson extrapolation on the trapezoidal rule, using every second station as the coarse grid. Report Q ± error, and state the number of significant figures the estimate supports.
  6. The velocity meter is accurate to ±3 % and depths to ±0.02 m. Using the sensitivity method of Lab 02, estimate the resulting uncertainty in Q. Compare it with the discretisation error from step 5, and state which dominates.

Part B — Work from a tensile test 25 pts

Generate the record with seed = the last two digits of your student number.

import numpy as np

def tensile_test(seed):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    d = np.linspace(0, 0.012, 240)                      # extension, m
    F = 1.9e6*d                                       # elastic
    yield_at = 0.0022
    m = d > yield_at
    F[m] = 1.9e6*yield_at + 1.6e5*(d[m] - yield_at) \
           - 7.0e6*(d[m] - yield_at)**2              # plastic, then necking
    F = np.maximum(F, 0) + rng.normal(0, 12, d.size)      # N
    return d, F
  1. Plot force against extension. Mark the yield point and the point of maximum force.
  2. Compute the total energy absorbed, in joules, by both the trapezoidal and Simpson rules. Report both and the difference between them.
  3. Compute the elastic energy stored up to the yield point, and the plastic energy dissipated after it. Report each as a percentage of the total.
  4. The specimen has a gauge length of 50 mm and a cross-sectional area of 78.5 mm². Convert your force–extension integral into an energy per unit volume, in J/mm³, and show the unit conversion explicitly.
  5. Repeat the total-energy calculation using only every fourth data point. Report the change. State how finely the machine would need to sample to keep the energy accurate to 1 %, and justify the answer with the order of the method.

Part C — RMS of a distorted waveform 25 pts

A supply voltage carries a third and a fifth harmonic:

v(t) = 311 sin(ωt) + 46 sin(3ωt + 0.4) + 21 sin(5ωt − 0.9)   V,   ω = 2π(60) rad/s
  1. Verify your rms function on a pure 311 V sine over exactly one period. It must return 219.9102 V. Report the value your code gives at n = 8, 16, 32, 64 and 128 intervals, to ten decimal places.
  2. Compute the RMS of the distorted waveform over one fundamental period with both the trapezoidal and Simpson rules, at n = 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256 and 1024. Tabulate the values and the error against the analytical result of task 4.
    Do not assume you will see a convergence curve

    Report exactly what you observe, even if the error does not change with n. If that happens, your task is to explain why — in terms of the integrand being periodic and the window being a whole number of periods — not to manufacture a ratio column. Fabricated convergence tables are marked as zero for this task.

  3. Compute the arithmetic mean of the waveform over the period. Explain in two sentences why it is not a useful measure of the supply, referring to how power depends on voltage.
  4. The RMS of a sum of harmonics is the root of the sum of the squares of the individual RMS values. Compute that analytically and compare it with your numerical result. Agreement to four significant figures is expected; state your agreement.
  5. Integrate over 0.9 of a period instead of a full one and report the RMS error introduced. Explain why an incomplete period biases the result, and state the practical requirement this places on a power analyser's sampling window.
Where marks are lost

Reporting a number without its unit, or reporting more significant figures than the input data or the discretisation error supports. Both are treated as errors of engineering practice, not of style.

Part D — AI Prompt Log 20 pts

Required prompt experiment — the wrong integrand

Ask an AI assistant, verbatim:

I measured depth and velocity across a river at 11 points.
Compute the average velocity and the flow rate in python.

Record its answer, then examine it against the following questions in your log:

  1. Did it compute the mean velocity as a plain arithmetic mean, or as Q/A? Quote the relevant line of its code.
  2. Did it assume equal spacing between stations? Test its function on the unequal spacing in Part A and report the numerical difference against your own implementation.
  3. Did it state any units? Did it check them?

Then write an improved prompt that supplies the station positions, states that spacing is unequal, and requires the discharge-weighted mean with a unit check. Compare the two answers and identify one remaining weakness in the second.

Conclude with two sentences on the general point: an AI can produce correct code for the wrong integrand, and no test of the code will reveal it. State what the engineer must therefore do.
