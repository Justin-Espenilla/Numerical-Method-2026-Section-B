Part A — The summation engine 30 pts

  1. A.1 Implement consolidation_U(T, es, max_terms) returning (U, terms_used, ea). It must stop on the approximate percent relative error, cap the term count, and handle T ≤ 0 as a special case.
  2. A.2 Reproduce the worked example: tabulate the term-by-term summation at T = 0.2 for m = 0 to 3, with columns M, M2, term, running sum, U and εa. Confirm U = 0.5041.
  3. A.3 Using the Scarborough criterion, state εs for 3, 4, 5 and 6 significant figures. For each, tabulate the number of terms required at T = 0.001, 0.01, 0.05, 0.2, 0.5 and 1.0. Present as a 4×6 table.
  4. A.4 Plot the number of terms required against T on a semilog axis for εs corresponding to 6 significant figures. Explain the shape in two sentences, referring to the exponent M2T.
  5. A.5 Verify your routine against the published values U = 0.5041 at T = 0.2 and U = 0.9313 at T = 1.0. Report the agreement to as many figures as it holds.

Part B — A settlement prediction 25 pts

An embankment is founded on a 6.0 m clay layer, drained top and bottom, with cv = 2.4 m²/year. The final primary settlement is 185 mm. Recall T = cvt/Hdr2, and that with two-way drainage Hdr is half the layer thickness.

  1. B.1 State Hdr and justify it in one sentence. This is the most common error in the whole assignment; an answer using Hdr = 6.0 m is wrong by a factor of four in time.
  2. B.2 Find T50, T90 and T95 by solving U(T) = 0.50, 0.90 and 0.95. Use a bisection search on T; state your bracket and tolerance. Compare with the standard values 0.197, 0.848 and 1.129.
  3. B.3 Convert each to a real time in months. Report how long until 90 % of the settlement has occurred, to a defensible precision.
  4. B.4 Plot settlement in millimetres against time in months from 0 to 60. Mark the 50 %, 90 % and 95 % points.
  5. B.5 The coefficient of consolidation from laboratory tests is uncertain by a factor of two either way, so take cv between 1.2 and 4.8 m²/year. Report the range of the 90 % time. Then state, in two sentences, how many significant figures your answer to B.3 really supports, and what that implies about summing the series to 10−10.

Part C — When a convergent series fails 25 pts

  1. C.1 Implement exp_series(x, n_terms) returning the sum and the largest single term encountered, using the recurrence termk = termk−1·x/k. No factorials.
  2. C.2 For x = −1, −5, −10, −20 and −30, tabulate: the series result, math.exp(x), the percent relative error, the largest term, and the ratio of largest term to final answer.
  3. C.3 Plot the percent relative error against the ratio in C.2 on log-log axes. Show that the number of significant figures lost is approximately log10 of that ratio.
  4. C.4 Fix it by computing e|x| and taking the reciprocal. Report the before and after errors at x = −20, and the number of significant figures recovered.
  5. C.5 Determine machine epsilon with your own halving loop. Report the value, the implied number of decimal digits, and the smallest tolerance it would be sensible to request in Part A.
Where marks are lost

Stating that "the series diverges" for negative x. It does not; it converges for every real x. The mathematics is sound and the arithmetic fails. Marks are for naming the mechanism — subtractive cancellation — and quantifying it with the largest-term ratio.

Part D — AI Prompt Log 20 pts

Required prompt experiment — the fixed term count

Send this prompt, word for word:

python function for terzaghi degree of consolidation U as a function of time factor T

Then, in the log:

  1. How many terms does the returned code sum? Is the count fixed, or driven by a convergence test? Quote the loop.
  2. Evaluate its function at T = 0.001 and compare with your own converged value. Report the error in percent. A fixed 10-term sum is typically wrong in the third figure there.
  3. Check its definition of M. A common error is M = π(2m+1)/2 written with the wrong index origin, so the sum starts at m = 1. Report what it used and the size of the resulting error.
  4. Write an improved prompt requiring a convergence-based stop, a term cap, and the returned term count. Compare the two answers.
  5. Name one defect that remains in the improved answer.
