Lab Assignment 10 · 150 points · Capstone

Capstone: Design of a Haunched Deck

One structure, analysed with every method in the course, verified at each stage, and finished with a section size you can defend. This assignment carries 150 points and replaces a final examination.

Due: two weeks Individual Python 3 AI Prompt Log (Word) required

The structure

A simply supported pedestrian deck spans L = 8.0 m. It carries a uniformly distributed load w = 24 kN/m, plus a point load P = 15 kN at x = 3.0 m representing a service pedestal. The section is rectangular, of width b = 0.40 m and depth d(x) haunched parabolically:

d(x) = d0 [ 1 + 0.5 (1 − 4(x/L − 0.5)2) ]     I(x) = b d(x)3/12

E = 25 GPa (concrete). Take d0 = 0.35 m as the starting depth. The serviceability limit is L/360.

Part A — Load effects by integration 25 pts

  1. A.1 Compute the support reactions by statics, by hand, and show the working.
  2. A.2 Build the shear diagram by cumulative integration of the load, and the moment diagram by cumulative integration of the shear, using your Lab 4 routine. Handle the point load explicitly and state how.
  3. A.3 Verify the moment diagram at three sections against hand statics. Tabulate hand value, computed value and difference.
  4. A.4 Report the maximum moment and its location. Confirm that shear is zero there, using your Lab 1 differentiator on the moment diagram, and state the relationship being tested.

Part B — Deflection by finite differences 35 pts

  1. B.1 Assemble the tridiagonal system for the haunched beam and solve it with your own Lab 6 Gauss elimination routine. Library solvers may be used only as a cross-check, and must be labelled as such.
  2. B.2 Verification first. Set d(x) constant and P = 0, and reproduce the exact result 5wL4/384EI. Report the error at n = 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, with the error-ratio column. The ratio must be 4.00; if it is not, your boundary treatment is wrong and must be fixed before continuing.
  3. B.3 Apply Richardson extrapolation to your two finest constant-EI results and report the improvement.
  4. B.4 Choose the mesh for the real analysis and justify it in writing from the convergence table. State the discretisation error of your chosen mesh.
  5. B.5 Solve the haunched beam with the point load. Report the maximum deflection, its location, and whether span/360 is satisfied at d0 = 0.35 m.
  6. B.6 Plot the deflected shape. Differentiate it to obtain rotations and report the rotation at each support in radians and in degrees.
  7. B.7 Solve the same system with Gauss–Seidel. Check diagonal dominance first, report the iterations required for a tolerance of 10−10, and confirm agreement with the direct solve. State which method you would use for a 5,000-node model and why.

Part C — Sensitivity and design 35 pts

  1. C.1 Using the Lab 2 method, compute the dimensionless sensitivity of the maximum deflection to w, P, L, E, b and d0. Present the tornado chart.
  2. C.2 Two of these sensitivities can be predicted exactly from the physics. Identify which, state the predicted values, and confirm your numerical results reproduce them. Explain why the sensitivity to L is not a clean integer for this loading.
  3. C.3 Find the depth d0 that exactly satisfies L/360, by Newton–Raphson on d0. Report the iteration table and the converged depth to the nearest millimetre.
  4. C.4 Solve the same design problem by bisection and confirm the two agree. Report both iteration counts.
  5. C.5 Concrete costs ₱ 6,800 per cubic metre. Compute the volume and cost of the deck at your design depth, and at depths 10 % above and below. State the cost of the safety margin.
  6. C.6 The load w is uncertain by ±15 % and E by ±20 %. Propagate these by root-sum-square and by a 10,000-sample Monte Carlo. Report the probability that the deck exceeds span/360 at your design depth, and recommend a final depth with that probability stated.

Part D — Written engineering judgement 25 pts

In the report, answer each in one short paragraph. These are marked on reasoning, not length.

  1. D.1 Your mesh has a discretisation error and your load has an uncertainty. Which is larger, by how much, and what does that imply about how much effort refining the mesh deserves?
  2. D.2 The haunch makes EI vary smoothly. If the section instead changed in a step at mid-span, what would happen to your convergence ratio, and why?
  3. D.3 You computed rotations by differentiating a numerical solution. Referring to Lab 1, explain why that quantity is less reliable than the deflection itself, and how you would check it.
  4. D.4 A colleague proposes skipping the constant-EI verification because "the haunched case is the one we need". Give the argument against, in terms a project manager would accept.
  5. D.5 State the final design: depth, deflection, mesh, discretisation error, exceedance probability, and cost. Three sentences, no hedging.

Part E — AI Prompt Log 30 pts

The capstone weights the log most heavily of any assignment, because the problem is large enough that AI assistance is genuinely useful and genuinely dangerous.

Required prompt experiment — the whole problem at once

Send this prompt, word for word:

write python code to find the maximum deflection of a simply supported beam
with varying EI using finite differences

Then, in the log:

  1. Run its code on the constant-EI case and compare against 5wL4/384EI. Report the error.
  2. Run the convergence study on its code. Report the error ratio. If it is not 4.00, find the defect and quote the line. A first-order boundary treatment is the usual cause and it is not visible without this test.
  3. Count how many of the following it did without being asked: imposed the boundary conditions correctly, used a centred second difference, produced a convergence check, stated the order, handled the point load. Report the count out of five.
  4. Write an improved prompt and compare. State one defect that remains.
  5. The judgement question. In three sentences: the AI's code ran without error and returned a plausible deflection. Explain precisely what would have to go wrong for a plausible but incorrect deflection to reach a construction drawing, and which single check in this assignment would have stopped it.

The one thing that fails this assignment outright

Reporting a design depth without the convergence evidence behind it. A number produced by an unverified mesh is not an engineering result, however carefully the rest of the report is written.
